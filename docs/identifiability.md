# Can the press force identify friction? (identifiability note)

Scope: a self-consistency study on a *verified* teaching-scale solver (`ringsim`). Nothing here is validated
against experiments. All numbers come from `results/identifiability/identifiability.json` unless another
file is named; the command that made it is stored inside each file.

**How to read the headline.** This is a method demonstration under stated assumptions: it shows how to quantify
what force and one diameter measurement each say about friction, in a model where the data are generated by the
same solver that is inverted. The "22x" below is a statement about *precision* (posterior width), not accuracy.
When the data are not generated by the inverted model, the posterior stays equally narrow but moves (see
"Model discrepancy"): a bore-gauge diameter in place of the mid-plane one biases m_f by 1.4 posterior standard
deviations at m_f 0.25 and 4.9 at m_f 0.1, and an unmodelled 5% additive force offset biases force-only m_f by
0.18 (true 0.25) and 0.35 (true 0.1) while force + ID is unaffected. The qualitative conclusion (force alone is
weak, one diameter is strong) survives; the numbers 0.0046 and 22x do not describe a real press.

## Question and model
A plant measures the die force during a ring compression and wants the friction factor m_f. The force is
`F(s) = K * F*(m_f; s)`: K lumps material strength and speed, F* is the solver's normalised force. The
inner-diameter (ID) change depends on m_f but not on K (checked below). Unknowns: (m_f, K).
K-independence of ID is a property of the model, not a finding: the formulation has no strain hardening and no
temperature, and friction is defined as a fraction of the flow stress (m_f times sigma_bar / sqrt 3), so K
cancels identically. This is textbook for ring tests; in a real forging with hardening, rate or temperature
effects, K and the shape are not exactly separable.

Setup (standard 6:3:2 ring, m = 0.15, to 30% reduction):
- Forward model: one solver sweep over 26 values of m_f (0 to 1), interpolated in m_f (PCHIP). K is analytic,
  so it costs no solver runs. Interpolation check, leaving out one node at a time for m_f 0.1-0.5: worst force
  error 0.58%, worst ID error 0.059 points.
- Hidden truth: m_f = 0.25, K = +7%. Data: force at 10 strokes (3-30%) with 1% noise each; optionally one ID
  measurement at 30% (sigma 0.3 points) or an independent K measurement (2%).
- Prior: m_f uniform on [0, 1]; log K normal with sigma 0.075 (about +-15% at two sigma). Posterior computed
  exactly on a 401 x 401 grid.

## Result 1: force alone leaves a ridge; one ID measurement collapses it
| Data | Posterior std of m_f (this realisation) | Mean over 100 noise draws | 90% interval of m_f |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| force only | 0.103 | 0.105 | 0.137 to 0.469 |
| force + K known (2%) | 0.100 | 0.070 | 0.194 to 0.525 |
| force + one ID measurement (0.3 pt) | 0.0046 | 0.0046 | 0.242 to 0.257 |

- Force only: m_f and K are strongly anti-correlated in the posterior (correlation -0.95), a thin curved
  ridge ([posterior_force_only.png](../results/identifiability/figures/posterior_force_only.png)). The posterior
  mean (0.33) sits on the ridge but 0.08 above the truth, within the interval.
- One ID measurement cuts the m_f std by a factor of 22.5 in this realisation (22.7 as the ratio of the 100-draw means) and pins K to 0.35%
  ([posterior_force_plus_id.png](../results/identifiability/figures/posterior_force_plus_id.png)).
- Knowing K to 2% helps only partly
  ([posterior_force_known_k.png](../results/identifiability/figures/posterior_force_known_k.png)). The headline
  "force is confounded with K" is therefore *incomplete*: even with K known, the force is simply not very
  sensitive to m_f. The table below shows why.

## Result 2: the friction-step table, recomputed from the sweep
Ratio of the force curves F*(b)/F*(a) over strokes 3-30%. "Lift" is the mean ratio minus 1; "shape change" is
what is left after dividing out one uniform scale (max deviation), i.e. what K could *not* imitate.

| Friction step | Mean force lift | Shape change (max) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0.0 to 0.1 | 7.8% | 1.6% |
| 0.2 to 0.3 | 3.4% | 0.13% |
| 0.3 to 0.5 | 2.7% | 1.6% |
| 0.5 to 1.0 (*) | 0.48% | 3.8% |

(*) Built from artefact-affected curves: above m_f about 0.55 the solver force has contact-artefact jumps (see Result 3), so this row mixes physics with discretisation noise.

At m_f 0.2-0.3 the whole force curve moves up almost uniformly (shape change 0.13%), which is exactly what a
7% change in K would do: that is the near-confounding. Above 0.3 the lift per step is small (2.7%, then
0.48%). These numbers replace an earlier planning estimate whose definition of "shape change" was not stated; use
the table above.

## Result 3: where is force informative? (`std_vs_true_mf.png`)
Posterior std of m_f against the true m_f ([figure](../results/identifiability/figures/std_vs_true_mf.png)):
- Force only: about 0.09-0.12 for true m_f from 0.15 to 0.5 (worse, 0.19, near zero friction where the prior
  edge and the ridge combine). Roughly flat, not a sharp feature at 0.3.
- Force + ID: 0.003 near m_f 0.05, rising to 0.008 at 0.5 and to about 0.008-0.03 above 0.6, because the ID
  curve flattens at high friction (change at 30%: -14.8% at m_f 0.5, -22.2% at 1.0).
- **Force is not monotone in m_f from about 0.45** (`force_at_30_not_monotone`): F*(30%) peaks at m_f 0.425
  (1.8283) and falls to 1.8223 at 0.5, before the artefact band. The force-only likelihood is therefore flat or
  two-humped over about 0.4-0.5, so force-only numbers there are not a unimodal width; the figure shades this
  band too (lighter grey from 0.45).
- **Limitation, not signal:** for m_f above about 0.55 the solver's force at 30% jumps between neighbouring m_f nodes, in both
  directions (+4.1, -6.3, +5.9, -8.0, +7.6% between nodes 0.5-0.6-0.7-0.8-0.9-1.0, from
  `contact_artefact_force_jump_pct_at_30`) when nodes land on the die (the contact artefact; e.g. 0.60 and 0.90 give spuriously small force-only std). I do not trust the
  force-only curve there and shade it in the figure. A remeshing or smoother contact treatment in the solver
  would be needed to study high friction by force.

## Sensitivity to analyst choices (force-only unless stated; mean std over 50 noise draws)
- Prior sigma of log K 0.03 / 0.075 / 0.15: 0.079 / 0.104 / 0.114. A tighter K prior helps, but it would have
  to be tighter than batch-to-batch variation to matter.
- ID sigma 0.1 / 0.3 / 1.0 points: m_f std 0.0015 / 0.0046 / 0.015.
- Force only up to 20% reduction: 0.146 (vs 0.105 up to 30%).
- Force noise 0.25 / 0.5 / 1 / 2%: force-only std 0.052 / 0.071 / 0.104 / 0.165 (force + ID stays 0.0046). Even
  at 0.25% force noise the force alone leaves 0.05, so the weakness is not a noise-level artefact.
- 1% force noise is independent per stroke. A common multiplicative sensor gain error is *exactly*
  confounded with K and is not modelled.

## Model discrepancy: data not generated by the inverted model (`model_discrepancy` in the JSON)
Noise-free synthetic data, so every number is pure bias: posterior mean of m_f minus truth, and that bias in units
of the posterior std (a ratio well above 1 means confidently wrong).

| Truth m_f | Case | Bias in m_f | Bias / posterior std |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.25 | baseline (force + ID, model-consistent) | 0.0001 | 0.02 |
| 0.25 | bore-minimum ID used, mid-plane assumed (ID differs by -0.41 pts) | 0.0065 | 1.4 |
| 0.10 | baseline | 0.0001 | 0.03 |
| 0.10 | bore-minimum ID used, mid-plane assumed (ID differs by -1.48 pts) | 0.0166 | 4.9 |

Force offset (an additive constant equal to 1, 2 or 5% of the mean force, not modelled; only multiplicative K is):

| Truth m_f | Offset | Force-only bias | Force-only bias / std | Force + ID bias |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.25 | 1% | 0.066 | 0.64 | 0.0001 |
| 0.25 | 2% | 0.088 | 0.79 | 0.0001 |
| 0.25 | 5% | 0.183 | 1.7 | 0.0000005 |
| 0.10 | 1% | 0.211 | 1.4 | 0.00002 |
| 0.10 | 2% | 0.246 | 1.5 | -0.00004 |
| 0.10 | 5% | 0.352 | 2.4 | -0.0002 |

Reading: (i) the ID route is not immune. The ID that a bore gauge reads (minimum of the inner surface) differs from
the mid-plane value by barrelling, and an unmodelled definitional difference of about a point turns a 0.005 std
into a bias several times larger. In practice the usable ID accuracy is set by this definitional discrepancy
(0.0065 and 0.0166 in m_f at truths 0.25 and 0.1), not by the 0.3-point gauge noise. (ii) Force offset makes force-only inference
confidently biased, not wider; force + ID is insensitive to it because the ID does not depend on force at all.
(iii) The inverse-crime direction flatters ID (a smooth deterministic curve) more than force (the same solver's
force is already noisy at the contact-artefact level); the force result is, if anything, conservative.

## Where to measure, and an unknown rate sensitivity m (`results/identifiability/where_to_measure.json`)
Question: which diameter measurement(s) pin friction best, when the strain-rate sensitivity m is also unknown
(uniform on the dataset range 0.05-0.30, marginalised) instead of fixed at 0.15? Solver sweep m_f in 0, 0.1, ...,
1 times m in {0.05, 0.10, 0.15, 0.225, 0.30} (standard ring, 44 new solver runs plus the cached m = 0.15 ones),
interpolated in m_f then m. Same force noise (1%), K prior and ID noise (0.3 pt per measurement) as above;
noise-free data (typical widths). Posterior std of m_f, truth m_f = 0.25 and m = 0.15:

| Data | m known (0.15) | m unknown |
| --- | --- | --- |
| force only | 0.091 | 0.140 |
| force + ID at 10% | 0.0168 | 0.0177 |
| force + ID at 20% | 0.0078 | 0.0090 |
| force + ID at 30% | 0.0046 | 0.0063 |
| force + ID at 10% and 30% | 0.0045 | 0.0062 |
| force + ID at 20% and 30% | 0.0040 | 0.0059 |
| force + ID at 10%, 20%, 30% | 0.0039 | 0.0058 |
| force + ID at 30% twice (independent noise) | 0.0033 | 0.0053 |

- **Measure at the largest reduction.** One ID at 30% is about 2.8 times tighter than at 10% (m unknown: 0.0063 vs
  0.0177; 3.6 times with m known). A second measurement at a different reduction adds little (0.0063 to 0.0059 for
  20% + 30%); a repeated 30% measurement does about as well, so the gain is mostly averaging, not new information
  in the stroke shape.
- **Unknown m costs a little at low friction and a lot at mid friction.** At truth m_f 0.25 the ID-at-30% std rises
  from 0.0046 to 0.0063; at truth 0.5 from 0.0079 to 0.0212 (a factor of 2.7), because the ID curve flattens
  there and m trades off against m_f. At truth 0.1 it barely moves (0.0034 either way). Force-only rises from 0.091
  to 0.140 at 0.25 and from 0.086 to 0.124 at 0.5. Truth m = 0.05 or 0.30 at m_f 0.25 give about the same
  ID-at-30% std (0.0060 and 0.0058).
- **m is only weakly identified** by these data (posterior std of m about 0.028 at m_f 0.25 with ID at 30%).
- **Correction after review:** an earlier version of this table dropped the m-dependent speed factor (V/h0)^m of
  the force (it assumed V/h0 = 1; for the solver's standard ring V/h0 = 1.5). With the factor kept, the m-unknown
  numbers moved by up to about 10% (most at truth 0.5 with ID at 30%, and force-only); the conclusions did not
  change. On a real press the size of this term depends on V/h0 in the units K is quoted in; it acts as an
  additional K-like confound.
- Caveats: interpolation in m, leaving the m = 0.15 node out: ID error up to 0.092 points;
  force error up to 1.5% for m_f up to 0.5 (up to 9.0% over all m_f, driven by the contact-artefact jumps above
  m_f 0.55). Standard ring only, noise-free data, uniform prior.

## Supporting checks
- **K invariance** (`results/gp/invariance_K.json`, full dataset; 10 extra solver runs at K = 0.6 and 1.6):
  ID differs from the K = 1 run by at most 1.9e-13 points; normalised force by 1.5e-14 relative; raw force equals
  K times the base. A GP with log K as an input learns an ID length scale of 75 (unit box, upper bound 1000)
  against 0.86-3.7 for the other inputs (48 times their median); for raw log force the K length scale stays
  finite (1.8). The ID length scale does not run to the bound, so "irrelevant" is read from the 1e-13 solver
  result, with the GP agreeing. These length scales come from the deterministic fit (2 threads, fixed seed,
  recorded in the file); fits with other thread counts move them by tens of percent; the conclusion does not.
- **Inverse friction** (`results/gp/inverse_friction.json`): brentq on the solver finds m_f for a target ID
  change of +10, -5, -15, -20% at 30% reduction: 0.106, 0.309, 0.507, 0.708 in 6, 7, 9, 10 solver calls
  (32 total), residual under 0.01 points. The same root on the full-data GP mean (no solver calls) lands within
  0.0002, 0.0035, 0.0016, 0.0040 of the solver root. The last target sits in the flat region, where a
  0.3-point measurement leaves a wide interval.

## Best ring for sensing friction (`results/design/design.json`, GP trained on the full dataset)
Objective: expected posterior std of m_f after one ID measurement (0.3 points) at 30% reduction, m = 0.15,
averaged over true m_f in [0.05, 0.95]; search box = training box. BoTorch (8 Sobol + 22 log-EI steps) finds
OD/ID 2.35, H/ID 0.81; a 21x21 grid search agrees on the ridge (2.36, 0.79; objective 0.01030 vs 0.01034 for
BO). Versus the standard 6:3:2 ring the objective falls from 0.0169 to 0.0103 (factor 1.64 on the GP). BoTorch is
not needed for a 2-D problem a grid solves; it is a pipeline demonstration, and the objective is flat along a
ridge, so the optimum's exact position (H/ID 0.79 vs 0.81 here, 0.78 in an earlier fit) is not meaningful.

GP-free solver check (11-value m_f sweep, 0 to 1, per ring, objective by PCHIP in m_f with no GP variance):
both rings completed 30% reduction at every m_f (`complete: true`), so both objectives are over the same range,
m_f 0 to 1, with no extrapolation: standard ring 0.0157, best ring 0.0093, factor 1.70. On the shorter range
0 to 0.9 the factors are 1.55 (solver). `design.py` now refuses to extrapolate: an incomplete sweep gets
`None` for the common-range objective and is reported separately as "extrapolated". Caution on fold-over: with an
earlier GP fit the best ring was H/ID 0.78 and it folded over at m_f = 1.0 (visible in the earlier `design.json`
in the repository history), while this fit's H/ID 0.81 ring does not. The best ring therefore sits near the
fold-over limit of this solver (no remeshing), and "the thicker, wider ring" is the robust conclusion, not the
specific optimum.

**The objective is dominated by high friction, so the "best ring" is mostly best at high friction.** In the
solver check the standard ring's per-truth posterior std runs from 0.0033 (true m_f 0.05) to 0.046 (0.85); truths
above m_f 0.6 contribute 76% of its average. Restricted to true m_f up to 0.6 the improvement factor is only 1.24
(solver) and 1.20 (GP); above 0.6 it is 1.92 (solver). For lubricated forging (m_f mostly below about 0.4) the
standard ring is nearly as good; the gain matters for poorly lubricated, high-friction work, which is also where
this solver is least reliable.

The best ring's ID at 30% runs from +19.5% at m_f 0 to -30.4% at m_f 1, the standard ring's from +19.5% to
-22.2%: more hole movement per unit friction, mostly at high friction. Limits: ranked on friction sensitivity
alone (no manufacturability or press-capacity limits), one measurement noise level, and the best ring sits
near the edge of the box in OD/ID. High-friction rows of the solver check are affected by the same contact
artefact as the force curves, which is why the ID rather than the force is used.

## First contact with experiment (`results/validation/ring_test_pop2023.json`)
What this is: a consistency check of the solver's friction model against one published dataset, not a validation.
Data caveat: two rows (dry and mineral oil at 30%) imply a ring volume 4 to 5% above the initial one, which metal
conservation rules out; most likely the outer diameter was measured at the barrel bulge, possibly a transcription
error (not checked against the original PDF). Only the inner diameter enters the fit; the weak outer-diameter check
below is affected.
Data: Pop, Neag, Sas-Boca, *Materials* 16(5), 2045 (2023), doi:10.3390/ma16052045, CC BY, Table 2, transcribed
verbatim into `data/external/pop2023_table2.yaml`: cold AA6068, 6:3:2 ring (15/30/10 mm), dry, mineral oil and
graphite-in-oil, height reductions 0.2/0.3/0.4. For every row the measured inner-diameter change
(d1i / d0i - 1) was inverted for the Tresca factor m_f by brentq on the solver (standard ring, rate exponent
m = 0.05, the dataset's lowest; 7-9 solver calls per row). The solver reached 40% reduction for every m_f up to
0.425 (it folds over earlier at higher friction), so all nine rows, including the three at 0.4, could be inverted.

| Lubricant | m_f at 0.2 / 0.3 / 0.4 | Mean m_f | Spread (max - min) | Authors' mean mu | m_f / (sqrt 3 mu) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Dry | 0.348 / 0.362 / 0.366 | 0.359 | 0.018 | 0.173 | 1.19 |
| Mineral oil | 0.273 / 0.282 / 0.252 | 0.269 | 0.030 | 0.083 | 1.86 |
| Graphite in oil | 0.247 / 0.259 / 0.241 | 0.249 | 0.017 | 0.078 | 1.83 |

- **One m_f per lubricant fits all three reductions to within 0.017-0.030.** That is a real (if modest) test of
  the friction model: the same factor must reproduce the hole at three strokes. The spread is larger than the
  reading-resolution interval (+-0.05 mm on d1i moves m_f by about +-0.003 to +-0.007 per row), so the model does
  not explain the three points perfectly; for mineral oil the 0.4 row sits low.
- **The ordering matches:** dry > mineral oil > graphite in oil, the same order as the authors' mu. Oil and
  graphite are close in both.
- **Against the authors' mu**, using the low-friction approximation m_f ~ sqrt(3) mu (it assumes the contact
  pressure is about the flow stress and von Mises yield): the inferred m_f is 1.2 times sqrt(3) mu for dry and about
  1.85 times for the lubricated cases. Their mu comes from a different friction law (Coulomb) read off a
  Male-Cockcroft chart computed with other assumptions, so agreement within a factor of two is all one can expect;
  that is what we see. It does not say which is right.
- **Rate sensitivity:** with m = 0.02 instead of 0.05 the inferred m_f drops by 0.008-0.026 (largest for dry).
  The solver accepted m = 0.02.
- **Bore minimum vs mid-plane:** at these frictions the hole's minimum is at the mid-plane, so the two definitions
  give the same m_f (within 0.0003).
- **Outer diameter at the inferred m_f** (secondary check, not used in the fit): predicted vs measured OD change
  differ by -1.7 to +2.0 points (for example dry 0.2: 8.5% vs 7.7%; mineral oil 0.3: 14.6% vs 16.3%), with no
  consistent sign. The solver's OD is the mid-plane bulge; the paper does not say where d1e was measured, and the
  measured OD values are not monotone across lubricants at fixed reduction, so this check is weak.
- **What it is not:** the material's flow curve and rate sensitivity are unknown here (no hardening in the
  solver), the friction law differs from the paper's, it is one alloy and one lab, and the comparison uses the
  same mesh as the dataset (nz = 8). It shows that the Tresca model with one factor per lubricant is
  consistent with real ring tests to a few hundredths in m_f, nothing more.
- Figure: [ring_test_pop2023.png](../results/validation/figures/ring_test_pop2023.png) (measured ID change vs
  reduction with the solver curves at each lubricant's mean m_f).

## What this means for the plant
Measure one part's inner diameter per batch or shift: it is what identifies friction. Force alone gives about
+-0.1 on a 0-1 scale (the uniform prior has std 0.289, `prior_std_mf`), useful as a weak prior and a drift
signal, not as a friction gauge.
